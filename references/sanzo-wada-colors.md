# Sanzo Wada color combinations

Use this reference when choosing color for a new expressive interface. The complete local [palette catalog](sanzo-wada-palettes.json) contains all 348 numbered combinations, each with two to four named colors and digital hex values. Search it by palette ID, color name, or hex; load only the records relevant to the task.

## Apply a combination to an interface

1. Start with the product's existing brand colors, content, and lighting. Use a Wada combination when it helps the desired mood; do not replace established tokens without a reason.
2. Choose one numbered combination and view its swatches together. Treat the combination as a relationship between colors, not a prescribed assignment to background, text, and buttons.
3. Assign roles deliberately: one color may become the action signal, while the others support surfaces, imagery, or accents. Neutral ink and paper colors may be added for readable text and quiet space. Keep decorative colors from posing as interactive controls.
4. Check text, icon, focus, and control contrast in the actual layout and in all states. Adjust a color's tone if needed and record the change; an accessible adaptation is better than a literal hex match.
5. Compare on screen and, if print fidelity matters, against the physical book. Digital hex values approximate printed inks and differ between conversions.

## Sources and color fidelity

- Sanzo Wada, *A Dictionary of Color Combinations*, Vol. 1: the numbered printed combinations are the visual reference. The book and PDF are not bundled into this skill.
- [Sanzo Wada's interactive dictionary](https://sanzo-wada.dmbk.io/): useful for browsing swatches visually. Its digital hex conversion can differ from the catalog below.
- The local catalog is derived from [Matt DesLauriers's corrected dataset](https://github.com/mattdesl/dictionary-of-colour-combinations), which provides 159 colors and 348 combinations with sRGB approximations. Its [MIT license](sanzo-wada-data-LICENSE.md) accompanies the data.
